Why it's leaking viewers

What the retention graph says

  • 85% of viewers are gone by 0:30. The title promises "THIS", but the first minute is a calm walkaround of the broken cart. The $600 is never said and nothing gives a reason to stay.
  • Flat after that. Viewers who make it past the intro mostly stay, so the intro is where to fix it.
  • A bump at the end. Likely people skipping to the reveal. They want the result, so the opening should promise it.
  • The voiceover stops at 1:05 and music runs for eight minutes. No story, no new questions, nothing to wait for.

The fix in one line each

  • Thumbnail: show the problem ($600, dead) and hold back part of the answer.
  • First 5 seconds: one strong hook, no dead air, music from frame one.
  • First 30 seconds: open the big question ("will it even turn on?") and a second one ("can a buffer save this body?").
  • Body: steps with a payoff every 60 to 90 seconds, a running "spent so far" number, and a short voice line at every step.
  • Ending: the power-on moment and the reveal, with real numbers.

Thumbnail ideas

Every image below is built from real frames of your footage, with nothing generated or altered on the cart. Colors come from the Sidequest logo.

What's working

Clean before/after at a matched angle, a bright "after", and the cart reads clearly even at small sizes.

What to change

  • It gives the whole answer away. The viewer gets the full story from the image, so there's no reason to click.
  • "BEFORE / AFTER" wastes the text. The split already says that. Use the words for the hook: $600, won't start.
  • The before doesn't look broken. It's bright and clean, so the change looks small.
  • Stock styling. Lime brush strokes are a template look, not your navy and orange.
  • The title repeats the image. "Into THIS" adds nothing when the image already shows "this".
  • Check the "after" is accurate. It looks heavily processed next to the raw frames.

Test these three first

  1. 1A2 Split golden: keeps the before/after you know works, adds the hook ($600 + WON'T START). The front-on golden-hour cart is the clearest object at phone size.
  2. 2B1 Dead cart: only the problem, with the empty battery bay as proof. Matches the new intro and holds back the payoff.
  3. 3C1 Hero street: the brightest frame in the video, with a price that feels too low for what you see.

YouTube Studio's Test & Compare runs up to three thumbnails against each other on watch time. Keep the title fixed while it runs. "I Bought a Golf Cart That Won't Even Turn On" works with all three, then test titles after you have a winner.

All of them at once

Three new intros

Built from research into how 44 breakout videos from the biggest build and restoration channels open. Thirty openings were drafted and scored blind by three judges, and these are the winners for three different hooks. Each runs about 25 seconds and leads into Step 1. The voice is AI (Google "Puck") for now; re-record it in your own voice with the same lines.

1The Bet

Opens on an object with a question attached. The price anchor (about ten grand new) tells a viewer who doesn't know golf carts why $600 is wild, and both outcomes are on the table.

This tail light is going to tell me if I wasted six hundred bucks, or got a ten thousand dollar golf cart for almost nothing. Because the day I got it, nothing on it lit up. 2012 Yamaha. Empty battery bay. No solenoid. Controller's not working. Sun-faded lights. A chalky body. Gray plastic trim. I'm buffing the body, not painting it. So here's the bet. If those tail lights glow and this thing drives, I win. If not, I just built a very clean six hundred dollar lawn ornament.

2The List

Price, model and dead state in the first two seconds, then "either I got a steal, or there's a reason it was six hundred". The checklist is the structure, and the last box is the one he's not sure about.

Six hundred dollar golf cart. 2012 Yamaha. Won't turn on. New, these are about ten grand. So either I got a steal, or there's a reason it was six hundred. Here's the reason. Batteries. There aren't any. Solenoid. Also missing. Controller. Dead. Plus a couple of broken wires I'm not counting as a box. Body. Oxidized, chipped, sun-faded. I'm buffing it, not repainting it. Four boxes I can probably handle. It's the last one I'm not sure about. Will this thing ever move again?

3From This

Pays off the title in the first sentence on the best shot you have, then rewinds. The twist (no repaint) and "I'm not a body shop" carry the personality.

I turned a six hundred dollar golf cart into this. New, one of these is about ten grand. Here's what six hundred bucks actually got me. A 2012 Yamaha that wouldn't even turn on. No batteries. No solenoid. And a dead controller. And the plastic looks like it's been in an oven. A body shop would paint it. I'm not a body shop. So the body just got a buffer. Even this gray trim came back black without paint. But none of that matters if it won't drive. Best case, it drives. Worst case, I spent six hundred bucks on a really shiny chair.

Why these work

  • Talking from the first frame. Breakout openings start speaking at about 0.25 seconds and say 75 to 110 words in the first 30 seconds. The old intro said its first real hook at 0:17.
  • Price plus a specific failure by 8 seconds, with a reference price so $600 means something.
  • One yes/no question the footage answers: will it drive? It does, at 8:37 in your original.
  • Assumptions in this mock-up: the solenoid and controller were replaced and the total spend was about $1,000. Change the lines if not.
